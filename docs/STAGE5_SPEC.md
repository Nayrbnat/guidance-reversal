# Stage 5 design: does segment-reporting transparency change the Guidance Reversal result?

**Status:** RUN (rev 4, 2026-11-03; results of the 2026-11-04 run are in section 11; the changes are listed in section 10). The design below was written and revised before any outcome was computed. **Exploratory**: it follows a null result in Stages 1-4 (see `MEMO.md`).
Items marked **(author)** were decided by the author; items marked **(my call)** were settled by the drafter and are listed together in section 9 so they can be checked.

## 1. Intent and why

Stages 1-4 find no evidence that executives who guide, sell and leave leave behind a company that underperforms. The motivating case, a large S&P 500 payments company whose departure
came in 2025, had very opaque segment reporting: from its 10-Ks and 10-Qs an outsider could not rebuild revenue as price x quantity. The idea is that when investors cannot see the business for
themselves they rely on management, so management's guidance carries more weight, and a sudden reversal after a departure hurts more.

The question: **is the (null) average effect hiding a pattern that only appears when a firm's reporting is opaque?**

## 2. The new variable: transparency score `T`

An integer 0-5 per firm-year, assigned by a language model reading the firm's 10-K, blind to returns, to the executive's exit, and to every other variable in the study. **(author)**

- **Higher means more transparent.** `T = 5` when the filing lets an outsider rebuild revenue as price x quantity. Opacity is `5 - T`. **(author)**
- **Rubric (draft; frozen at the end of calibration, section 6).** The score reflects what the filing lets a reader reconstruct, not how long it is. **It is neutral to the number of segments (my call, rev 2):** a single-segment firm that discloses volumes and prices scores high, and a many-segment firm that discloses only dollar figures scores low. The scorer is told not to reward or penalise segment count, and the rubric is worded in terms of disclosed drivers, so that `T` measures disclosure quality and not the firm's opportunity or need to disclose (see "Construct validity" below).

**The score is weighted by where the cash flow comes from, not by revenue (my call, rev 2).** Disclosing price and quantity for a line that is a small part of the economics does not make a firm transparent. In a razor-and-blade business (equipment sold at thin margin, a recurring consumable or injectable earning most of the profit), P x Q for the equipment with only a dollar figure for the consumable is *opaque*, because most of the cash flow is unmodelled. A price or quantity counts as disclosed when it is **stated or can be derived** by dividing two disclosed figures for the same line and period (author, rev 3): wireless revenue divided by wireless connections gives revenue per connection. The scorer therefore first identifies the **profit engines** (the lines, products or contracts that generate the bulk of gross or operating profit and recurring cash flow), using segment profit and margin where disclosed and otherwise the filing's own language (consumables, aftermarket, installed base, attach rates, subscriptions, royalties, float or interest income, high-margin mix), and then judges what share of that profit pool the disclosed prices and quantities let an outsider rebuild. Coverage is measured against the profit pool, not against revenue. **If the filing does not even let a reader tell where the profit comes from, that is itself opacity and caps `T` at 2.**

| T | What the 10-K disclosure lets an outsider do |
|---|---|
| 5 | Rebuild, as price x quantity, the lines that generate essentially all of the profit and recurring cash flow (volumes such as units, subscribers, procedures, bookings, volumes processed; prices such as average selling price, rates, take rates, attach rates) |
| 4 | Do that for the lines behind most (roughly three quarters or more) of the profit pool; the rest disclosed only as dollars |
| 3 | Do that for roughly half the profit pool, or see price *or* quantity (not both) for the main profit engine |
| 2 | See revenue and profit by line or segment, but no drivers for the main profit engine; **this includes the razor-and-blade case**: drivers disclosed only for a minor or low-margin line while the high-margin recurring line is dollars only. Also the cap when the engine cannot be identified |
| 1 | See revenue only in total or by geography, with at most a few drivers named in prose and no figures |
| 0 | See one undifferentiated revenue figure: no breakdown and no drivers at all |

The scorer returns a short structured reply (its identified profit engines, whether price and quantity are disclosed for each, and the integer `T`), so the audit can check the engine identification and not only the final number. `T` remains the integer; the engine fields are stored for audit and for a reliability check on whether two reads pick the same main engine. **Scope limit:** this reconstructs the revenue and gross-profit engine; it does not model capital expenditure, working capital or taxes, so it measures transparency of the earnings drivers, not of free cash flow.

- **Timing (no look-ahead). (author)** For a departure event, `T` is from the last 10-K *filed strictly before* the announcement date `dep_ann`. For a CEO-quarter in the Stage 3 panel, the last 10-K filed on or before the quarter's start.
- **What the scorer reads. (author, rev 3: all of the company's materials)** For a firm-year, the 10-K and **everything else the company had furnished to the SEC in the 12 months up to that 10-K's filing date**: the 10-Qs and the earnings releases and investor presentations attached to its 8-Ks (exhibits 99). From the 10-K: Item 1 (first 8,000 characters), the segment note and MD&A segment text, and the operating-statistics windows (seat miles, carloads, subscribers, procedures and the like), capped at 40,000 characters; plus up to 2 10-Qs (4,000 characters each) and up to 4 8-K exhibits (6,000 characters each), so at most about 72,000 characters per read. **Timing:** nothing filed after the 10-K is ever included, so the timing rule (the last 10-K strictly before the announcement) keeps its no-look-ahead guarantee. **Limit:** investor presentations that exist only on a company's website (not furnished to the SEC) are not included: they cannot be timestamped or fetched reproducibly. Earnings-call transcripts are not included. **Accepted by the author (2026-11-02): the study uses EDGAR-furnished materials only.** On the 20-company calibration set, 5 of the 6 disagreements between the model and the author's scores are one-point differences in judgement; 1 (one firm) traces to a website-only deck (the January 2020 Source Book). This is reported as a limitation: a firm that publishes its operating data only in website decks scores lower than its true transparency.
- **Extraction outcomes. (my call, rev 2)** Three classes. `ok`: a segment note or MD&A segment discussion was found. `limited_disclosure`: Item 1 was found and the filing either states a single reportable segment or has no segment note; **this is scored**, because thin segment disclosure is exactly what `T` measures and excluding it would select on the outcome being measured. `extraction_failed`: no identifiable 10-K body could be located (a technical failure); the only class excluded, and it is counted and reported by year and industry. Before the extraction is frozen, a stratified set of 30 real filings (spread across industries, eras and segment structures) is read against its extracted text.
- **Construct validity. (my call, rev 2)** `T` could partly proxy for business complexity, size, industry reporting conventions or changes in disclosure rules over time. Before any outcome is examined, the pilot report relates `T` to the number of segments (Compustat segments), log assets, SIC division and filing year, and reports how much of the variance of `T` industry and year explain. Sensitivities fixed in advance (listed in the freeze file): add log assets and segment count, interacted like `T`, to the T2 and T3 regressions; compare within SIC division (T1 placebo strata); compare within size tercile.
- **Blindness to identity. (my call)** Company name, ticker and executive names are replaced with placeholders before scoring. A model may still recognise a firm, so a leakage check is part of the audit: on a sample, the scorer is asked to name the company and year, and the recognition rate is reported as a limitation.
- **Reliability. (my call)** Every filing gets **two independent reads** (the same rubric, two differently worded prompts). The final score is the mean of the two, rounded half up; a gap of two or more triggers a third read and the final is the median. Report quadratic-weighted kappa between reads, the share within one point, and the distribution of `T`.
- **One measurement pipeline. (my call, rev 2)** The calibration, the pilot and the full run all use the **same scorer**: Claude Code in headless mode (`claude -p`) on the author's subscription, with one pinned model, the two prompt templates, one extraction version and one rubric. Headless mode has no temperature setting, so run-to-run variation is measured, not controlled: it shows up in the agreement between the two reads. These are hashed together into a measurement config and frozen; every stored score carries the config hash and the hash of its input text, and the runner rejects scores produced under any other config. A reliability figure from a different scorer would not validate the production one.
- **Hand-audit. (my call, rev 2)** Two separate samples, both disjoint from calibration and from each other **by filing accession number** (not just event id). (a) A **representative** audit: 100 filings drawn at random from the pilot, no stratification; the model-versus-author kappa for the gate is computed on this sample only, with a bootstrap interval, and it estimates reliability in the pilot population. (b) A **diagnostic** audit of 30, stratified across `T` levels and sectors, used to find rubric weaknesses and reported separately, never in the gate. The author reads a capped excerpt (8,000 characters of the segment and KPI text, the part after Item 1 in the extracted text, so the author reads what the model read) with model scores hidden. Realistic budget: about 3 to 4 minutes per excerpt, so 6 to 7 hours for the representative 100 and about 2 hours for the diagnostic 30, in separate sittings. If that is too much, the representative sample may be cut to no fewer than 60, which widens the kappa interval and is reported as such.

## 3. Hypothesis **(author)**

**H5.** The negative risk-adjusted return of guide-sell-leave firms is larger (more negative) when transparency is low. Equivalently: the interaction of the Guidance Reversal pattern with opacity is negative for returns.
The reverse reading (opacity alone predicts exits) is **not** a test in this stage.

## 4. Tests **(author: all three existing ones)**

All keep every other specification identical to the earlier stage. In every test, "supports H5" means the pattern is stronger at low `T`.

| Test | Extends | What is added |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | Stage 1 calendar-time alpha | Flagged firms split by `T` group; four-factor alpha per group; low minus high |
| T2 | Stage 3 P1 (exit probability) | `T` interacted with the P1 term |
| T3 | Stage 3 P2 (realised earnings miss) | `T` interacted with the P2 term |
| T4 | Stage 3 P3 (leavers vs stayers alpha) | The leaver-minus-stayer difference by `T` group |

**What the tests must be able to reject. (my call, rev 2)** H5 is a claim about *heterogeneity*: the effect differs between low and high `T`. The null is therefore "the two groups have the same effect" (which includes both being the same non-zero value), not "both effects are zero". T1 and T4 are built around the contrast itself, and each is checked in simulation to reject at about 5% when both groups carry the same planted effect of -10% a year.

**Groups. (my call)**
- **Cut points** are the 33rd (lower interpolation) and 67th (higher interpolation) percentiles of `T` over all S&P 1500 departure events that have a scored 10-K, fixed before any outcome is examined. Rule `p33_p67_v2`: low = `T` at or below the lower cut, high = at or above the upper cut, medium otherwise; an integer value goes entirely to one group. **If the two cuts coincide at level `c`** (a six-level score can pile up in one value), low = `T < c`, high = `T > c`, medium = `T == c`, so a contrast still exists. The motivating firm is scored only after the measurement is frozen and is not in the calibration set.
- **Group viability.** The tercile tests run only if low and high each hold at least 8 flagged events and each group's non-flagged placebo pool is at least 5 times its flagged count. Viability is decided **at run time from the full scores** (outcome-blind counts of flagged events and placebo pools per group), not at the pilot: a 200-filing pilot of non-flagged filings cannot show how many flagged events fall in each group. If viability fails, T1 and T4 are declared untestable, skipped with the reason recorded in the results, and left out of the Holm family (nothing is dropped, because no outcome has been seen); T2, T3 and the continuous analyses still run.
- **Continuous corroboration.** Because a six-level score loses information in terciles, each test is also reported with `T` standardised as `T_z`, as a continuous interaction. These are corroborating and outside the Holm family.
- **Standardisation.** `T_z = (T - mu) / sd`, with `mu` and `sd` computed once over unique scored filings (one row per accession, so quarterly repeats do not count more than once) and used in every dataset.

**T1 inference. (my call, rev 2)**
- **Placebo.** For each group, placebo portfolios are drawn from non-flagged departures in the *same `T` group* (matched on announcement-year counts, as before), so baseline alpha differences across `T` cannot masquerade as an effect.
- **Statistic and p-value.** The primary statistic is the difference in excess alpha: (flagged alpha minus the group's placebo mean) at low `T`, minus the same at high `T`. The p-value comes from a *centred* placebo distribution (each group's placebo alpha minus its own mean, then low minus high), which is the null of equal excess effects; a common shift in both groups cancels. The confidence interval is a calendar-block bootstrap (resampling announcement quarters), which respects overlapping windows and calendar-time dependence. Secondary: the alpha of the low-minus-high long-short calendar-time series with Newey-West standard errors. The same rule is applied to the broader "guidance + sale, any exit type" group (267 firms, Stage 2 S6) for power, reported alongside.

**T2 and T3 regressions. (my call)**
- Keep the Stage 3 firm and year-quarter fixed effects and two-way clustered standard errors. `T` varies by firm-year, so it is not fully absorbed; its main effect is still included.
- Include **all lower-order terms**: `T`, `T x optimism`, `T x sale`, and the original terms, plus the three-way `T x optimism x sale` for P1 and its P2 analogue. `T` is standardised (`T_z`, above) so coefficients are comparable; the effect per one point of `T` is also reported.
- **The H5 test is the coefficient on the three-way term**, oriented so that a positive value supports H5 (the implementation handles the sign convention of each model). Tests are one-sided in the H5 direction, as the earlier stages were, and the two-sided result is shown too. The complexity sensitivity (log assets and segment count entered alongside `T`) is reported beside each.

**T4 inference. (my call, rev 2)** The leaver-minus-stayer difference by `T` group, `D_low - D_high`, is tested with a **pooled event-level interaction regression**: the 252-day factor-adjusted abnormal return on `leaver`, a `high` indicator and their product, with standard errors clustered by announcement quarter and the p-value from a wild cluster bootstrap that imposes the null on the interaction only (so equal non-zero leaver effects are the null). Secondary: the calendar-time `D_low - D_high` with a bootstrap recentred at the observed contrast. Not used: independent label permutations within each group, whose null is "no leaver effect in either group", a different and stronger hypothesis than H5.

**T2 and T3 regressions. (my call)**
- Keep the Stage 3 firm and year-quarter fixed effects and two-way clustered standard errors. `T` varies by firm-year, so it is not fully absorbed; its main effect is still included.
- Include **all lower-order terms**: `T`, `T x optimism`, `T x sale`, and the original terms, plus the three-way `T x optimism x sale` for P1 and its P2 analogue. `T` is standardised (mean 0, SD 1) so coefficients are comparable; the effect per one point of `T` is also reported.
- **The H5 test is the coefficient on the three-way term**, oriented so that a positive value supports H5 (the implementation handles the sign convention of each model). Tests are one-sided in the H5 direction, as the earlier stages were, and the two-sided result is shown too.
- T4 compares the leaver-minus-stayer alpha difference between low and high `T` groups with the same cut rule as T1.

## 5. Reporting

**The results are filed whatever they show; there is no dropping rule. (author)** Each test reports its estimate, confidence interval, p-value and the smallest effect it could detect. Every table is labelled exploratory.

- **Multiple testing. (my call)** T1-T4 (those that are run; see group viability) form their own Holm family, each using its primary p-value (T1: the centred placebo contrast; T2 and T3: the three-way terms; T4: the pooled interaction). The 267-firm run of T1, the continuous analyses and the complexity sensitivities are reported as secondary and are not in the family. The project-wide count of tests across all stages (about 28 before Stage 5, plus these four) is disclosed beside the table. Earlier Holm families are not reopened.
- **Power.** 42 flagged events split three ways leaves about 14 per group. Each test reports its minimum detectable effect before the estimate is interpreted.
- **The motivating firm.** Its 2025 departure lies inside the data but has no complete 12-month return window, so it is a descriptive case study only and is not part of any test.

## 6. Build order and gate (needs the author's approval, see section 9)

0. **Extraction check.** Read 30 stratified extracted filings against their sources and freeze the extraction version (section 2).
1. **Calibrate (no returns, no outcomes).** On a small calibration set of firms that are not flagged events and not the motivating firm, choose the model and both prompt wordings and tune the rubric; then **freeze the whole measurement config (model, prompts, temperature, extraction version, rubric) and the group rule** in a commit.
2. **Pilot (with the production scorer).** Score about 200 filings, disjoint from calibration by accession, with both reads and the same config the full run will use; run the representative hand-audit (100) and the diagnostic audit (30) and the leakage check; measure cost per filing; relate `T` to complexity, size, industry and year. Report the cost of scoring the full set before any scaling.
3. **Reliability gate (my call, thresholds proposed; the author confirms before the freeze).** Reliability criteria, all required: weighted kappa between reads at least 0.70; within-one-point agreement at least 90%; weighted kappa between the model and the representative hand-audit at least 0.60; no two adjacent `T` levels hold more than 80% of firm-years. **Group viability** (section 4: distinct groups, at least 8 flagged events per group, placebo pools at least 5 times the flagged count) is not part of the pilot gate; it is checked by the runner on the full scores and decides whether T1 and T4 run. The pilot report shows the `T` distribution and the group sizes for information only. The gate file records the config hash, and the runner only accepts scores produced under that same hash.
   If reliability fails: revise the rubric on the calibration set only, re-freeze under a new rubric version, and re-pilot once on a new disjoint sample. If the second pilot fails, **stop, publish the reliability report, and do not run T1-T4.** No outcome has been computed at that point, so nothing is being "dropped".
4. **Scale.** Score the full set: for the Stage 3 panel essentially every S&P 1500 firm-year in the guidance-issuing sample, which is tens of thousands of readings including the second reads. The cost estimate from step 2 is shown to the author before this step starts.
5. **Run T1-T4**, then write up.

## 7. Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

- **Goals.** A defensible, reproducible `T`; four tests that answer H5 honestly; a clear statement of what the sample can and cannot detect.
- **Non-goals.** Re-opening or re-running Stages 1-4; changing the flag definition; widening the universe beyond the S&P 1500 **(author)**; causal claims; publishing per-firm scores.
- **Anti-goals (a failure even if it runs).** Any look-ahead (a 10-K filed after the announcement); tuning the rubric or cut rule after seeing outcomes; reading a significant result as confirmation without the power caveat; letting `T` leak into the flag.

## 8. Constraints and left to the builder

- Universe: S&P 1500 CEO/CFO departures announced 2004-2025 (as before). **Membership should be point-in-time (my call, rev 2):** the earlier stages use Execucomp coverage as a proxy for the S&P 1500; the build checks for a historical constituent table, reports how many events change if it is used, and otherwise records the limitation. The gvkey-to-CIK link is validated against its date ranges (identifier changes, mergers, spin-offs) and spot-checked by company name. Data: WRDS, as before. Environment: the repo's `uv` environment. Per-firm scores and filing text stay out of the public repository (licensing, named-executive material); only aggregate results are committed. New code lives in `src/`, outputs under `outputs/`, run from the repo root.
- **Left to the builder:** the source of 10-K text (EDGAR directly or a WRDS SEC table), the gvkey-to-CIK link and the handling of firms with no filing before an announcement; prompt wording within the rubric and model choice; batching and cost control; the exact audit sampling; file and function layout following the existing `gg_*.py` conventions, with unit tests for the non-model parts (timing rule, merge, agreement statistics).

**Rev 3 (2026-11-02), two author decisions.** (1) A price or quantity that can be derived from two disclosed numbers for the same line and period counts as disclosed (section 2, rubric). (2) The scorer reads all of the company's materials furnished to the SEC in the 12 months up to the 10-K (10-Qs, earnings releases, investor presentations attached to 8-Ks), not the 10-K alone, with the no-look-ahead timing kept by bundling only documents filed on or before the 10-K (section 2). **Rev 4 (2026-11-03), gate passed on the hand audit; author decision to keep rubric r3.** The 100-filing representative audit (hand scores by the author) gives weighted kappa 0.772 against the model (95% bootstrap CI 0.672 to 0.851; gate 0.60), within-one 92%. ## 11. Results (final run 2026-11-04 after the independent review's fixes, frozen config 72fb2dc9d4c9, rubric r3, extraction ex8, claude-haiku-5-5; 5,000 permutations, seed 0)

**Verdict: H5 is not supported. No test is significant; after Holm over the four tests every adjusted p is 1.0. The Stage 1-4 null does not hide a pattern in opaque firms.**

| Test | Estimate | One-sided p (H5 direction) | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 calendar-time alpha, flagged vs placebo, low vs high T | DiD +0.283 (annualised); low-T excess +0.092 (24 flagged), high-T excess -0.191 (18 flagged) | 0.961 | Sign opposite to H5 |
| T2 exit probability, T x O x S | -0.040 (se 0.309), per T point -0.039 | 0.449 | 1,943 exits, 85 clusters |
| T3 earnings miss, T x O x S x X | -0.452 (se 0.251), two-sided p 0.075 | 0.962 | 1,006 cells with S and X |
| T4 leaver minus stayer, low vs high T | +0.004 (se 0.044) | 0.462 | 81 and 65 leavers |

Secondary (outside the family): T1 continuous one-sided p 0.81; T4 continuous p 0.25 and calendar-time p 0.44; the complexity sensitivities for T2 and T3 were skipped because log assets and segment count are not in the Stage 3 panels. The project-wide test count is 32.

**Reading.** T1 and T3 point the opposite way from H5: flagged firms did worse where reporting is transparent, not where it is opaque. These two are not corrected for the 32 project-wide tests, rest on 18 and 24 flagged events (T1) and are an unregistered direction, so they are exploratory and should not be read as a finding. T1's bootstrap interval for the DiD (0.048 to 0.520) excludes zero on the wrong side, which is the reason to state it.

**Limitations, as ledgered.** (1) The model scores above the author's hand audit in 42 of 100 rows (mean +0.5), kappa 0.77; fours and fives are blurred. (2) Phases carry one score across their filings; first versus last filing in a phase differ by 0.5 points on average (kappa 0.68), which is noise that weakens every effect. (3) EDGAR-furnished materials only; website-only decks and transcripts are out. (4) The 1,525 MAJOR revenue-split changes were triaged on label lists alone and 95% were confirmed, so phases are probably over-split (cost, not bias). (5) Point-in-time S&P membership could not be verified. (6) Company tickers were not stripped from the pilot text (leakage not re-checked). (7) Only 42 flagged events exist, so T1 and T4 group sizes are small.

**Leakage check (2026-11-04).** 58 scored phase-first bundles, name-stripped as scored, were given to the same model with the question "which company wrote this?". It named the company correctly in 55.2% (32 of 58) and never answered UNKNOWN. Recognition is through residual identifiers the name list does not catch (brands, subsidiaries, trade names). Recognised firms scored 0.36 higher on average (3.09 vs 2.73, n 32 vs 26, not significant). The scorer sees only the disclosure text and no outcomes, so the channel to the tested outcomes is indirect, but it cannot be ruled out that outside knowledge of a firm leaks into T. Rescoring with a broader strip was judged out of proportion to the null result (about 80M tokens) and is recorded as limitation 8 of section 11.

**Superseded first run and the review's fixes.** A first run the same day, before an independent code review, gave the same verdict (family p T1 0.965, T2 0.448, T3 0.963, T4 0.462; Holm all 1.0; file `outputs/results/stage5_results_PRE_REVIEW.json`). The review found and we fixed, each test-first: (1) T1 dated T by the raw rule date while the return windows start on the annotator's announcement date (62 of 5,187 events differ, 47 earlier by a median of 61 days); (2) the T1 placebo pool lacked Stage 2's rule that the announcement be identified (the low-T pool fell from 2,272 to 1,649, the high-T pool from 2,131 to 1,531, flagged groups unchanged at 24 and 18); (3) the score assembly kept the highest of several reads instead of the read on the current text, and the input-hash check was never switched on in production (now every score is checked against the text it was made on: 0 stale reads); (4) Panel B now takes the last 10-K filed strictly before the release day. The corrections changed no conclusion: DiD 0.287 to 0.283, all four family p-values within 0.004.
