# Notes for Stage 11: what the arXiv review says, what I take from it, what I leave

Read 2026-11-07 (abstracts and search summaries only; no outside code was downloaded or run). The Stage 11 code is written from scratch from the textbook definitions, not from any repository.

| Idea | Source (arXiv) | What I take | What I do not take |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tests for a few large effects among many small ones | Donoho and Jin, *Higher criticism for detecting sparse heterogeneous mixtures* (math/0410072); Liu and Xie, *Cauchy combination test* (1808.09011); *Effective positive Cauchy combination test* (2410.10345) | HC and the positive Cauchy statistic on event-level lower-tail p-values; calibrate by placebo draws, not asymptotics | The analytic Cauchy p-value (it relies on dependence assumptions I cannot verify for overlapping return windows); large p-values cancel small ones in the plain Cauchy statistic, hence the positive variant |
| Corrections for model-scored variables | Battaglia et al., *Inference for regression with variables generated by AI or ML* (2402.15585); *Attenuation bias with latent predictors* (2507.22218); *When predictions become regressors* (2608.02909); Audinet de Pieuchon et al., DSL vs PPI (2506.09627) | The idea that a small human-labelled sample calibrates a model score; regression calibration as the simplest valid version | Prediction-powered inference and design-based supervised learning: with 100 labels they add validity, not power; split-sample instruments need repeated independent measurements I do not have |
| Evidence for the null | Bayesian model-averaged meta-analysis (2110.01076); Bayes factors in meta-analysis (2511.22535); heterogeneity priors (2007.08352) | A Bayes factor with a prior scaled to a stated smallest effect of interest; report sensitivity to the scale | Pooling the Stage 1 to 10 estimates in one meta-analysis: the stages share firms and sales, so they are not independent |
| Moving the clock | (own reasoning; the arXiv review found no paper on small-sample event-study power) | Test where the effect should appear: the reaction to the first new guidance | Anything claiming the date the guided period resolves: the cached actuals have no announcement dates |
| Few treated units | Alvarez, Ferman and Wuthrich (2504.19841); synthetic DiD (1812.09970) | Confirms that placebo-type inference is the right default for the 42-firm test | Synthetic controls for each event: gains uncertain, not run |
| Not used | Double machine learning (1608.00060), causal forests (1510.04342), Firth (2101.07620), empirical Bayes (2404.11198), tail modelling (2605.27474, a single-author preprint) | None | Low payoff here, or too easy to create false findings on weak data |

**Honest limits of the review:** abstracts only; several 2026 papers are single-author preprints I cannot vouch for; the foundational papers (higher criticism, Cauchy test, double machine learning, causal forests, Firth) are well established.
