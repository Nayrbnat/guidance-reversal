# Stage 10: a pre-registered test of the large-firm lead, on sales not yet seen

Status: **written 2026-11-07, before any data that could test it exists on this account.** Author: Nayrb (requested 2026-11-07: "lean on large firms"). **This is a pre-registration, not a run.** Nothing is computed until the trigger in Section 5 is met.

## 1. Why this, and what it is not

Large firms is the one subgroup that points the same way in two samples: Stage 8 (2004 to 2024, 102,606 insider-days) gave -0.265 (standard error 0.164, one-sided p 0.055) and Stage 9 (2025, 3,345 insider-days) gave -2.00 (standard error 1.07, one-sided p 0.031). That is **not** a finding, for three reasons, fixed here so they are not forgotten:

1. **Selection.** Large firms was chosen for Stage 9 *because* it came close in Stage 8. Its Stage 9 result is therefore not an independent test of an a-priori hypothesis, and pooling the two (about -0.30, one-sided p about 0.03) would double-count the selection.
2. **The two groups are not the same.** Stage 8 and Stage 9 each split at their own sample median market cap: about $4.8 billion in Stage 8 but about $18 billion in Stage 9 (79% of Stage 9 insider-days are above the Stage 8 median, because 2025 has more large firms among scored filers). "Large" meant different firms in the two runs.
3. **Power.** Stage 9's estimate is about eight times Stage 8's with a standard error of 1.07; at that precision a significant estimate is likely inflated.

A plausible story exists (the Stage 5 transparency score is built from revenue disclosure that matters more, and is read more reliably by the scorer, for firms with segment reporting, and large firms have more analyst coverage so opacity could matter differently), but I did not predict it before Stage 8 and will not present it as a mechanism until a clean test supports it.

## 2. The hypothesis and test (fixed now)

**H5-L.** Among officers and directors at **large firms**, the avoided loss after an open-market sale is larger where transparency is low: $\theta_L<0$.

- **Large firm, defined once, in dollars:** market cap the day before the sale of **at least $4.83 billion** (the Stage 8 primary-sample median, log market cap 22.298, computed from market cap alone before any outcome was looked at for this stage). It is **not** re-estimated on new data.
- Everything else as in Stage 9: insider-days of at least $100,000 by officers and directors, outcome $Y=-100\times CAR(+1,+63)$ with pre-sale four-factor betas, winsorised at 1% and 99% on the new sample, $Z$ with the Stage 5 mean and standard deviation, controls as in Stage 8, one-sided, $p<0.05$.
- **Design change from Stages 8 and 9, chosen on standard errors alone (Section 2a):** **SIC two-digit industry-by-year-quarter fixed effects** (one effect per industry and quarter) in place of separate industry and quarter effects, with standard errors **clustered by firm only**, whatever the number of quarters.

### 2a. Why the design changed, and what it did and did not rely on

A comparison of design variants on the Stage 8 panel (`scripts/se_bakeoff_stage8.py`, results in `outputs/results/stage8_se_bakeoff.json`) printed only standard errors, sample sizes and minimum detectable effects; **no coefficient was printed or saved**, so the choice could not depend on the answer. On 204,174 insider-days: baseline 0.152, firm-only clustering 0.130, industry-by-quarter effects with firm clustering **0.123**, flexible controls 0.132, industry-by-quarter plus flexible controls 0.124. Two further variants were rejected: dividing the outcome by the stock's own volatility changes the units and gives no gain in standard-deviation units (0.0226 against 0.0240), and shorter or longer horizons change the question. Against the Stage 9 design (firm clustering, separate effects) the gain is about 6% in the standard error (0.130 to 0.123), measured on the large Stage 8 panel; it may differ on a smaller new sample, so the sample-size thresholds in Section 5 are **left at their conservative values**. Stages 8 and 9 are **not** to be re-run under this design and reported; at most a sensitivity table, labelled exploratory.
- One confirmatory test. The project-wide count of formal tests becomes 38.

## 3. What counts as new data

Sale dates **after 2025-09-30**. The 2025-01 to 2025-09 sales are used up: their large-firm result has been seen. Sales from 2025-10-01 need CRSP prices through 63 trading days later; this account's CRSP v2 file ends 2025-12-31, so the first usable sales are those with prices available, which today is none beyond 2025-09-30. **The test cannot run until CRSP 2026 prices are on the account** (WRDS releases annual updates; check `crsp.dsf_v2` for dates after 2025-12-31 and the Fama-French file, which already runs to 2026-09-30).

## 4. Power, fixed now so the stopping rule is not chosen after seeing data

The large-firm coefficient needs a standard error of about 0.40 (minimum detectable effect about 1.0, still 4 times Stage 8's -0.265, because this is what a one-to-two-year sample can reach). Scaling Stage 9's large-firm standard error (1.07 on 3,345 insider-days) says that needs **about 24,000 large-firm insider-days with a score**, roughly the 2025-10 to 2027-09 flow. A smaller sample than that cannot test the lead and the result would be reported as uninformative whatever its p-value.

## 5. Trigger and rules

- **Run once, when** (a) CRSP prices cover the 63-day outcome window of every sale in the sample, and (b) the sample has at least 15,000 large-firm insider-days with a score (so the standard error is about 0.50 and the minimum detectable effect about 1.2; below that the test is not run and the lead stays a lead).
- Print the sample, the standard error and the minimum detectable effect **before** the estimate, as in Stages 8 and 9.
- **Supported:** $\hat\theta_L<0$, one-sided $p<0.05$. **Not supported:** anything else, reported with the minimum detectable effect. A pooled estimate with Stage 8 is descriptive only.
- No other subgroup is run on the new data. The Stage 9 guidance interaction (-2.26) is a second lead; it is **not** part of this test and, if the author wants it tested, needs its own pre-registered line in a separate spec.

## 6. What to do in the meantime

Nothing is leaned on in the write-up: the memo reports large firms as a lead from two samples chosen in sequence. Two actions are free of look-ahead and useful now: (1) score the roughly 29% of Stage 8 insider-days without $T$, which raises power for the large-firm test (and for the Stage 8 test itself) without touching new outcomes, and (2) pull the detectability moderators the cache lacks (analyst coverage, trading volume, institutional ownership), which the literature suggests matter most for large firms. Neither changes the hypothesis in Section 2.

## 7. Amendment 1 (2026-11-08): an early, underpowered run, plus a pooled test

**Written before any 2026 insider trade, return or regression was touched.** Made at the author's instruction, after I reported that the Section 5 trigger (at least 15,000 scored large-firm insider-days) would not be met until about 2028. I am recording honestly that this amendment exists because the author wants an answer sooner; it does not make the test better powered. What I looked at since Section 6 was written, none of it an outcome: the end dates of CRSP (2025-12-31), the Fama-French factors (2026-09-30) and Compustat daily prices (2026-11-06); the 2025 monthly counts of large-firm insider-days (about 590 a month with a score); and which filters the WRDS REST API honours.

**What changes.** Section 5's trigger is waived **for this run only**. Sections 1 to 4 (hypothesis, the $4.83 billion cut-off, design C, variables) stand. The full-power test remains pre-registered and, when the sample reaches 15,000, will use **only sales after 2026-06-30**, so it stays independent of this run.

**New data and prices.** Sales dates 2025-10-01 to 2026-06-30, the last dates whose 63-trading-day windows are complete in both the price and the factor files. Prices: CRSP v2 through 2025-12-31 and, after that date only, returns built from Compustat daily security prices (`comp.secd`, `src/gg_compustat_px.py`: split-adjusted price times the total-return factor, primary US-dollar common issue). **The Compustat route is used only if it validates against CRSP on the 2024 to 2025 overlap: correlation of daily returns at least 0.99 and at least 95% of returns within 0.001.** If it fails, this run is cancelled, not loosened. Compustat has no delisting returns; sale-days whose firm delists inside the window are dropped and their share reported. Sales in the new window link with the CRSP name rows that were still active on 2025-12-31 treated as active through 2026; the share of new sales that link is reported against the 2025 link rate.

**Two formal tests, Holm-corrected together** (the project-wide count becomes 39, from 37):
- **T-A (H5-L, large firms).** Market cap at least $4.83 billion the day before the sale; sales 2025-10-01 to 2026-06-30 **only**; design C; $\theta_L<0$, one-sided.
- **T-B (pooled, all firms).** All officers and directors, sales 2025-01-01 to 2026-06-30 (the Stage 9 months plus the new months); design C; $\theta<0$, one-sided. The Stage 9 months were analysed in Stage 9 (coefficient $-0.20$, standard error 0.73), so this is a cumulative test, not an independent one; the new-months-only estimate is reported beside it.

**Power, printed before either estimate:** standard error, minimum detectable effect and a firm-cluster bootstrap for each test. My expectation: minimum detectable effect near 2 for T-A and near 1.3 for T-B, both far above the Stage 8 estimates of $-0.27$ and $-0.13$.

**Interpretation (fixed now).** Supported: the Holm-adjusted p-value below 0.05 for the test in question. Not supported: anything else, reported with the minimum detectable effect; at these minimum detectable effects a non-rejection says almost nothing. A significant result at a coefficient far above Stage 8's (for example below $-1$) will be reported as a probable overestimate and a lead for the full-power run, not as a finding. No other subgroup or specification is run on the new months.

## 8. Amendment 2 (2026-11-09): the validation gate failed because of an implementation error, now corrected; the bar is unchanged

**Written before the corrected validation was run on the data that decides the gate.** Amendment 1 fixed a bar (correlation of daily returns with CRSP at least 0.99 and at least 95% of returns within 0.001, over the 2024 to 2025 overlap) and said a failure cancels the run. The first validation failed (correlation 0.9883; 98.9% within 0.001) and the chain cancelled itself. A read-only diagnosis, using no outcomes, found the cause: **the CRSP-Compustat link table cached here has no issue-ID column, so the converter's "primary issue" map was empty and it kept every share class of each firm** (30 of 2,148 firms have two or three issues), and the validation then compared **every** issue's returns against the single CRSP security of the firm. Six rows of one firm differed by more than 0.5; removing only them gave a correlation of 0.994. Amendment 1 had intended one issue per CRSP security ("the primary US-dollar common issue"); the implementation did not do that.

**The correction (an implementation fix, not a looser bar).** For each CRSP security (permno), the Compustat issue is chosen by the share of days its returns match the CRSP security's returns **on 2024 only** (within 0.001; at least 100 matching-candidate days), and the security is **dropped if even its best issue matches on less than 95% of 2024 days**. The gate is then tested on **2025 only, which was not used to choose the issue**: the same bar (correlation at least 0.99, at least 95% within 0.001). A pre-run diagnostic on the same data (the first one I ran, before this amendment was written) gave 99.98% within 0.001 and a correlation of 0.9997 out of sample, so I expect the gate to pass; it is re-run by the code and the run is cancelled if it does not pass. The firm-level splice then uses the chosen (permno, issue) pairs, so dual-class firms get the right series for each permno. **I am recording that this change followed a failed gate and that its design used knowledge of the failure; it uses only validation data, never outcomes.** Compustat securities for which no CRSP overlap exists (listed after 2025-01) cannot have an issue chosen this way and are dropped unless the firm has exactly one issue.

**Universe note (added with Amendment 2, before any outcome).** The insider table streamed for 2026 covers every firm; the cached file used for 2004 to 2025 covers only the original firm universe (the firms whose CUSIPs the guidance panel selected, about 3,000). Using all firms in 2026 would change the sample against Stage 9 and would need the 10-K history of roughly a thousand firms never scored. **The 2026 sales are therefore restricted to the same CUSIP universe as the cached file.** The Compustat prices were pulled for the wider set, which is a superset and harmless.

## 9. Results of the interim run (2026-11-09; Amendments 1 and 2 and the universe note applied; run once by `scripts/stage10_chain.sh`, output `outputs/results/stage10_results.json`)

- **Gate (Amendment 2).** 2025 out of sample, one Compustat issue per CRSP security chosen on 2024: correlation 0.99999, 99.997% of returns within 0.001, no row differing by more than 0.05, prices within 0.1% for 99.9996% of rows. Passed. (The first gate, which compared every issue of each firm with one CRSP security, failed at correlation 0.9883; see Section 8.)
- **Data.** 2026 insider sales restricted to the original CUSIP universe; 1,101 filings labelled, 46 new phase starts scored with the frozen scorer (config hash 72fb2dc9d4c9...); 0 of 26,097 existing filings changed phase or score. Linked insider-days 14,844 (2025-01 to 2026-06); with T and an outcome 14,317 from 986 firms; the new months 7,627 from 864 firms; large firms in the new months 5,956 from 619 firms. Missing T: 337 no prior filing, 91 not scored, 84 extraction failed.
- **Power (printed before the estimates).** T-A: standard error 3.16/2.49 = 1.271, minimum detectable effect **3.16** pp per sd of T (my expectation was about 2). T-B: standard error 0.780, minimum detectable effect **1.94** (expected about 1.3). Both worse than expected. Simulated rejection at the minimum detectable effect: 83% (T-A) and 53% (T-B); the 53% for T-B means the planted effect at "minimum detectable" was not reliably detected, a rough figure from 30 replications.
- **T-A (large firms, new months, design C).** Z = -0.311 (se 1.271), one-sided p 0.403, Holm-adjusted 0.807, 90% interval [-2.40, +1.78], n 5,956. **Not supported.**
- **T-B (pooled all firms, 2025-01 to 2026-06, design C).** Z = +0.283 (se 0.780), one-sided p 0.641, Holm-adjusted 0.807, 90% interval [-1.00, +1.57], n 14,317. **Not supported** (wrong sign).
- **Beside T-B, new months only, all firms:** Z = +0.664 (se 1.035), one-sided p 0.739, n 7,627.
- **Reading under Section 7.** Both tests are far from significant and the intervals comfortably contain both zero and the Stage 8 estimates (-0.13 all firms, -0.27 large firms); with these minimum detectable effects the run can neither confirm nor rule out effects of that size. It rules out only effects of roughly -2 or stronger for large firms and -1 or stronger overall. **The large-firm lead is not confirmed**; it stays a lead for the full-power run (sales after 2026-06-30, at least 15,000 scored large-firm insider-days).
